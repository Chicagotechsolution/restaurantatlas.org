Method

Why trust a guide from a site you've never heard of?

You shouldn't — until you can check its work. Every Restaurant Atlas guide publishes with its sources attached: the contracts we read, the fee schedules we compared, and where the numbers came from. If a figure is an estimate, it says so in the sentence, not a footnote.

Nobody pays to be recommended. When a guide names vendors, it names the trade-offs too.

  1. Collect the paperReal contracts, rate cards, and statements from operators who share them.
  2. Interview operatorsOwners and managers review drafts against their own numbers.
  3. Edit for the pass lineIf it can't be read between lunch and prep, it gets shorter.
  4. Date it and revisit itEvery guide carries its last-checked date; stale guides get flagged, not quietly left up.
